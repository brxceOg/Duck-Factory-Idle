# Quack & Co. Industrial Complex — Source of Truth

**Status:** Foundational design specification  
**Project type:** Mobile-friendly browser game / idle management tycoon  
**Repository:** `brxceOg/Duck-Factory-Idle`  
**Primary audience:** Abby  
**Canonical rule:** When implementation notes, prototypes, or older discussions conflict with this document, this document wins until it is deliberately revised.

## 1. High concept

**Quack & Co. Industrial Complex** is a goofy, cartoon-driven idle management game in which Abby runs an enormous high-tech factory dedicated to manufacturing hyper-specific sentient rubber ducks for a demanding global market.

The land and its industrial potential were inherited by Brice and given to Abby as a gift. The game should feel like a playful, personal present—not a generic tycoon reskin. Its humor comes from treating absurd duck products and sentient factory mishaps with completely serious corporate confidence.

Example products include **Cyberpunk Ducky**, **Existential Dread Ducky**, and **Chef Ducky**. Strange production accidents can create valuable hybrid ducks, open secret niche markets, and turn a routine production line into an unexpected discovery.

## 2. Product goals and platform

- Build a playable game for modern mobile browsers, especially iPhone Safari.
- Host the project through GitHub Pages when configured.
- Make the factory readable and enjoyable on small screens without sacrificing the sense of a large industrial complex.
- Support smooth scrolling across a very large, expandable map.
- Make progress satisfying both during active play and while idle.
- Keep gameplay systems modular and documented so future sessions can extend the game without contradicting established decisions.
- Treat the repository as the implementation source of truth. Update this document when a design decision changes.

## 3. Core player fantasy

The player is the boss of a wildly over-engineered rubber-duck empire. They expand a plot of land, install machines and conveyor belts, tune production, discover bizarre duck variants, and turn increasingly ridiculous products into a profitable operation.

The world should be silly, colorful, and full of life. Machinery looks powerful and overbuilt; ducks look glossy, expressive, and slightly too sentient for comfort. The business interface treats every disaster as a solvable quarterly opportunity.

## 4. Core gameplay loop

1. **Generate Quack.** Factory activity produces sound-wave energy, represented as the primary currency, Quack.
2. **Invest.** Spend Quack to purchase and place machines, conveyors, production modules, and other factory equipment.
3. **Produce ducks.** Equipment turns inputs and production capacity into duck products.
4. **Sell to markets.** Standard duck lines earn revenue based on product type, throughput, and relevant upgrades.
5. **Research and upgrade.** Invest in a skill tree to improve throughput, efficiency, market value, and mutation opportunities.
6. **Discover mutations.** The Mutation Vat occasionally creates unusual hybrids that can unlock hidden, high-paying niche markets.
7. **Expand.** Unlock more land and extend production lines into a larger, more complex facility.
8. **Repeat.** Reinvest profits to build a louder, stranger, more efficient duck empire.

This is the intended high-level loop. Exact costs, rates, timers, and balancing values are implementation details to tune during playtesting, not fixed values in this initial document.

## 5. The Quack economy

Quack is the signature currency. It is generated by sound waves from operating factory equipment: more active machinery and suitable upgrades increase the factory's collective output.

### Design requirements
- Present Quack as a clear, readable number in the primary HUD.
- Give the sound-wave concept a strong visual identity through wave pulses, meters, ripples, or compact decibel-style feedback.
- Make it intuitive that upgrading and operating machines can increase Quack generation.
- Keep currency feedback legible and restrained enough not to overwhelm the factory view.
- Avoid requiring the player to listen to audio; the economy must be understandable with sound muted.
- Balance the economy so that purchases feel meaningful, early progress is brisk, and later expansion creates longer-term goals.

Quack is a fictional game resource, not a simulation of scientifically accurate sound-pressure measurements. A decibel display can be used as flavor, but should not mislead the player into thinking real-world dB math is governing the economy unless that is explicitly implemented.

## 6. Factory construction

The player spends currency to acquire and place different equipment, including:

- Duck-production machines and specialized product lines.
- Conveyor belts and junctions that connect factory stations.
- Input/output stations, storage, and transfer equipment as needed by the production model.
- Upgraded or specialized machinery with stronger output or unique effects.
- Mutation equipment, especially the Mutation Vat.
- Decorative and world-building elements where useful.

### Placement and readability
- Placement must work on touchscreens, with generous tap targets and clear selection states.
- Factory objects should align consistently to a placement grid or another explicit placement system.
- Conveyors must communicate their direction and connections clearly.
- Placement previews should show valid and invalid positions before the player commits.
- The camera must scroll smoothly in all supported directions as the map expands.
- The UI should not obscure the area the player is trying to build on; mobile controls may collapse into compact panels or drawers.
- Avoid clipping, unintended overlaps, and equipment that becomes unreadable at typical phone scale.

The precise production simulation—such as whether every duck physically travels through each conveyor or whether some lines are abstracted—must be decided during implementation and documented before the system becomes difficult to change.

## 7. Sentient ducks and product lines

Ducks are both the factory's products and its personality. Each product family should have a recognizable silhouette, face, color palette, props, markings, and animation identity.

Initial examples:
- **Cyberpunk Ducky:** neon accents, tech accessories, and a futuristic personality.
- **Existential Dread Ducky:** tired eyes, gloomy expression, and absurdly bleak packaging or market copy.
- **Chef Ducky:** chef hat, kitchen-themed details, and a proud culinary expression.

These are examples, not an exhaustive list. New duck concepts should be specific, visually distinct, and funny at a glance. Avoid making every variant a simple recolor.

### Duck asset baseline
Use the supplied glossy yellow rubber-duck reference as the primary style guide:
- Bright yellow/orange body with shiny highlights and warm gradients.
- Bold, dark reddish-brown outline.
- Large, expressive eyes and a prominent orange-red bill.
- Rounded, toy-like proportions and exaggerated cartoon expressions.
- Strong readability at small sizes.

The supplied reference is watermarked. It is a style reference only; do not ship the watermarked image as a final game asset. Create original assets that follow the desired visual language.

## 8. Mutation Vat

The Mutation Vat is a signature system that occasionally malfunctions or produces unusual hybrids. Mutations should feel like delightful, sometimes alarming discoveries rather than ordinary incremental upgrades.

Examples include a duck with toaster wings or other improbable combinations of duck anatomy, household objects, industrial parts, and themed accessories.

### Intended behavior
- Mutations can occur through a chance-based process associated with the vat and/or relevant upgrades.
- Some mutations unlock hidden, specialized markets.
- Secret markets offer higher value in exchange for rarer or more demanding product requirements.
- Mutation-related upgrades can improve occurrence chance, discovery outcomes, or value.
- Discoveries should receive distinctive visual and UI feedback.
- Maintain a collection/discovery record so players can revisit what they have found.

Exact odds, rarity tiers, and rewards remain to be balanced. Do not make mutation chance so high that discoveries stop feeling special, or so low that a player can play for long periods without meaningful feedback.

## 9. Markets and progression

The global market demands hyper-specific duck products. Different market niches should reward players for diversifying their production and discovering mutations.

Potential market dimensions include:
- Standard demand for common duck lines.
- Specialized demand for themed ducks.
- Secret demand unlocked by unusual hybrid discoveries.
- Improved payouts from research, production quality, or market-specific bonuses.

The game should communicate why a product is valuable and what the player needs to do to access a new market. Avoid hidden requirements with no clue or feedback.

A concrete market roster, unlock conditions, prices, and refresh cadence will be defined during balancing.

## 10. Skill tree

A dedicated skill tree provides long-term choices. It should include upgrade paths such as:

- **Production:** faster machines, improved throughput, or increased output.
- **Quack acoustics:** more Quack generated by active factory equipment.
- **Mutation research:** increased mutation opportunities and improved discovery potential.
- **Market intelligence:** improved payouts, new niche markets, or better demand.
- **Logistics:** more efficient conveyors, connections, or factory-wide operations.
- **Expansion:** lower expansion costs or access to additional land.

These are initial categories, not a locked final tree. Skill nodes must communicate their cost, effect, prerequisites, and current status. The tree should reward specialization without allowing one path to make all others irrelevant.

## 11. Large, scrollable map

The facility occupies a large, expandable plot of land. The player should be able to pan around smoothly and inspect both the current production area and distant expansion space.

### Requirements
- Use a consistent world coordinate and placement system.
- Keep camera movement smooth and predictable on touch and pointer input.
- Prevent accidental camera movement when interacting with UI controls.
- Provide a clear visual distinction between unlocked land, purchasable land, and inaccessible land.
- Preserve object positions and map state when the player scrolls away and returns.
- Keep performance in mind as the factory grows; avoid doing unnecessary work for every off-screen object.
- Provide useful orientation cues if the world becomes large enough that navigation is difficult.

The map should feel spacious and coherent, not like an endless flat grid with no visual character.

## 12. Art direction and UI

The canonical visual details live in [ART_DIRECTION.md](./ART_DIRECTION.md). In brief:
- Goofy, wacky, expressive cartoon art.
- Beautiful top-down map tiles and carefully composed factory scenery.
- Glossy yellow rubber-duck characters with bold dark outlines.
- Warm orange, amber, brown, and gold UI frames with beveled, tactile buttons.
- Consistent visual language across world assets, machines, ducks, icons, and panels.

Use the supplied images as art-direction references, not as final watermarked assets. Prioritize cohesive, original assets over unrelated stock imagery.

## 13. Interface principles

The main screen should keep the factory as the star. Persistent information should be compact and immediately readable.

Likely interface areas:
- Quack balance and generation rate.
- Factory status and key production information.
- Build/equipment menu.
- Upgrade and skill-tree access.
- Mutation/discovery collection.
- Market or sales information.
- Settings, save status, and help.

These are functional requirements, not a mandate to show every panel simultaneously. Use layered menus, drawers, or contextual panels where appropriate on mobile.

UI controls must be functional, have clear pressed/selected/disabled states, and remain usable with touch. Do not leave decorative buttons that look interactive but do nothing.

## 14. Saving and resilience

- Save meaningful player progress locally in the browser.
- Persist currency, purchased upgrades, unlocked areas, placed equipment, discovered ducks, and other core progression.
- Use a versioned save structure so future updates can migrate older saves safely.
- Avoid silently overwriting valid saves after a loading error.
- Provide a deliberate reset option with confirmation.
- Clearly communicate save failures when the platform allows it.
- Test reloads and mobile browser behavior, including the possibility of a tab being suspended.

Offline earnings may be added if the implementation supports them reliably. If used, they should have clear rules and reasonable caps; do not assume background browser timers run continuously.

## 15. Technical and development principles

- Target modern mobile browsers first; desktop should remain usable for development and playtesting.
- Prefer modular, readable code and keep simulation logic separate from rendering and UI.
- Use a consistent world-to-screen coordinate transform for the scrolling map.
- Optimize for stable frame rate and responsive touch input.
- Avoid introducing external asset dependencies without a clear reason; keep licensing and attribution requirements documented.
- Do not use watermarked reference images as shipped assets.
- Verify actual behavior in a browser after major changes rather than treating a successful commit as proof that a feature works.
- Keep changes focused, test key interactions, and update documentation when behavior or design decisions change.

The exact implementation stack can be chosen during the initial technical setup. Do not introduce a complex framework without a concrete need.

## 16. Accessibility and usability

- Important information must not rely on color alone.
- Maintain adequate text contrast against decorative panels.
- Keep touch targets large enough for phone use.
- Provide readable labels and helpful feedback for purchases, unlocks, and failures.
- Ensure the Quack economy remains understandable with audio muted.
- Avoid excessive flashing, motion, or sound effects; settings should eventually allow reducing or muting effects.

## 17. Initial milestone sequence

1. **Project foundation:** establish the browser entry point, repository structure, responsive layout, and save foundation.
2. **World and camera:** create the top-down map, placement grid, pan/scroll controls, and land boundaries.
3. **First production line:** add a starter duck machine, conveyors or a documented simplified production model, and a visible production loop.
4. **Quack economy:** connect machine operation to Quack generation and implement the first purchases.
5. **Factory construction:** add equipment selection, placement, validation, and basic expansion.
6. **Markets:** sell the first duck products and display income and demand feedback.
7. **Mutation Vat:** introduce mutation rolls, original hybrid variants, and discovery feedback.
8. **Skill tree:** implement production and mutation upgrades with prerequisites.
9. **Content and polish:** add more duck families, machines, animation, effects, and world detail.
10. **Mobile and persistence testing:** verify scrolling, touch controls, save/load, responsive UI, and performance on a phone-sized viewport.

This order may be adjusted when technical dependencies require it, but each milestone should leave the project in a playable state.

## 18. Definition of done for a feature

A feature is not complete merely because its code exists. It should:
- Work in the intended browser context.
- Have usable UI feedback and handle invalid states.
- Fit the established art and interaction direction.
- Persist its state when applicable.
- Avoid breaking existing controls or progression.
- Be tested at a phone-sized viewport.
- Have relevant documentation updated when it changes the source of truth.

## 19. Open decisions

The following require deliberate decisions during development:
- Whether production is fully simulated duck-by-duck, partly abstracted, or hybrid.
- Exact machine categories, costs, throughput, and upgrade curves.
- The starting machine, initial Quack amount, and first-session tutorial.
- Market refresh and demand mechanics.
- Mutation odds, rarity tiers, and discovery rewards.
- Land expansion pricing and total practical world scale.
- Offline progress rules.
- Audio implementation and how sound feedback behaves on mobile.
- Final code architecture and asset format.

Do not silently treat any of these open decisions as already finalized.

---

**North star:** Build a funny, beautiful, mobile-friendly duck factory where every expansion makes the operation more impressive, every mutation has the potential to be wonderfully wrong, and Abby always has another strange business opportunity to chase.

## 20. Non-negotiable implementation and delivery rules

These rules apply to every coding session and commit. They are mandatory, not optional preferences.

### Finish implemented code; do not leave placeholders
- When implementing a feature, complete the code for the agreed scope rather than leaving placeholder sections, stub functions, TODO-only implementations, mock controls, or comments promising that core behavior will be added later.
- Do not present a feature as complete if any part of its agreed functionality is still a placeholder.
- Avoid fake interactions: every visible control introduced for a feature must perform its intended action or be clearly and deliberately disabled with an explanation.
- If a requirement cannot be completed safely in the current change, explain the limitation before calling the feature done; do not disguise unfinished work as a finished implementation.
- Temporary scaffolding is allowed only when explicitly identified as temporary and agreed upon. Replace it before the relevant feature is declared complete.
- Before committing, review the changed files for TODOs, stubs, unimplemented branches, placeholder content, dead controls, and missing error handling related to the change.

### Provide a playable test link after every commit
- After **every commit that changes the game**, provide the user with a direct link to the playable build so they can test the current state.
- Prefer the deployed GitHub Pages URL once GitHub Pages is configured and deployment has succeeded. Verify the URL and deployment status before claiming the link is live.
- If GitHub Pages is not configured or the latest commit has not deployed successfully, do not invent or falsely label a playable URL. Clearly state that a live build is not yet available and provide the most useful verified alternative, such as the repository or commit link, plus the steps/blocker needed to make the build playable.
- Do not imply that a commit itself proves the game works. When possible, verify the build and test the affected interactions before reporting it as ready.
- In the post-commit response, include: a concise summary of what changed, the commit link, the playable link (or a clear note that it is not yet available and why), and any relevant test results or known limitations.
- Keep the link easy to find; do not bury it in a long explanation.

These delivery rules exist to keep every change testable and prevent unfinished code from silently accumulating.
